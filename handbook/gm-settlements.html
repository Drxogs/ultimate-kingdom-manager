The capital comes first

Until there's a capital, no events, desk matters or audiences arrive and no projects can start. Found it in Setup → The capital: pick a settlement already on the Kingmaker map, or name a new one, and you'll get a task to claim the hex and draw it.

The map and the Settlements tab

The Settlements tab keeps each settlement's tracks; the map shows where it is. The two are matched by name, so name the map feature exactly as the players named it.

  • A settlement you draw on the map joins the tab when you save the map, with Homes to match its size (a town comes with Tenements).
  • Renaming a settlement in Setup doesn't rename it on the map, so rename the label with the hex tool too.
  • The picture at the top uses the terrain of the settlement's hex.

Your tools on the Settlements tab

  • − and + on a track: lower or raise it a stage for free, with no roll. Use them for what was standing before the kingdom started, for damage from events, or to fix a mistake.
  • Lightning bolt: draw a trouble card for that settlement now.
  • Crown: make it the capital. Waves: mark it as waterside (the Great Bridge needs water). Bin: remove it from the realm (the map isn't changed).

Trouble

At the start of each month, a settlement missing something its size needs has about a one-in-three chance, per missing track, of drawing a trouble card about it. Tenements add a one-in-four chance of crime or fire. There's no trouble in a kingdom's first three months. The cards are listed on the players' Settlement Tracks page.

Several trouble cards would make a good scene at the table (undead in an unblessed graveyard, a gang's warehouse). Feel free to play one out, then record the result with Record outcome.

Pinnacle perks you run

A few pinnacles do things you handle yourself: the Arena's games, the Counting House's cheaper loans, Embassy Row's extra answer at audiences, the Shadow Network's early warning, the Watchtowers' bonus, and the Knightly Order and University extras. Each one's text says what to do.
