The capital

A new kingdom's first job is to found its capital. The GM does it while setting up the kingdom; tell them where it stands and what it's called. It starts as a village with Huts, a Campfire and a Shrine.

How a settlement grows

Each settlement grows along twelve tracks, two for each realm stat. Each track runs from a starter to +1, +2 and +3, and every stage has its own description, so you can watch a campfire become a tavern and then a grand inn.

Stat (seat) Tracks
Community (Viceroy) Homes, Food
Culture (Counselor) Faith, Performance
Economy (Treasurer) Commerce, Industry
Relations (Emissary) Governance, Watch
Military (General) Walls, Army
Arcane (Magister) Learning, Public Works
  • Homes sets the size: Huts make a village, Tenements a town (from kingdom level 3), Neighborhoods a city (level 9), Boroughs a metropolis (level 15).
  • No other track can get more than one stage ahead of Homes. A village can build up to +1; a town up to +2.

On the Settlements tab, open a settlement's Tracks to see each one: what stands now, what it gives, and what comes next. The Build or Upgrade button belongs to that track's seat.

What tracks give

Stages +1 to +3 give that much as a bonus to their skills on checks about that settlement. (For cards that aren't about a settlement, the best settlement's bonus counts.) Starters open a track but give no bonus, and Homes gives none at all.

Track Skills
Food Agriculture, Wilderness, Exploration
Faith Folklore
Performance Arts
Commerce Trade, Boating
Industry Industry
Governance Politics, Statecraft
Watch Intrigue
Walls Defense
Army Warfare
Learning Magic, Scholarship
Public Works Engineering

Many stages do more besides: markets and inns earn BP, workshops make building cheaper, a church calms the people when it opens, a magistrate's court keeps crime down. Each row says what it gives.

Building

Building a stage is a project on that track's desk: delegate it or handle it personally like any other. With the council on, you can propose it instead.

Stage Cost Work
Starter 2 BP 3 days
+1 8 BP 7 days
+2 25 BP 14 days
+3 60 BP 21 days
Pinnacle 120 BP 28 days

Pinnacles are the great works: a University, a Monastery, a Great Bridge. Once a metropolis has a track at +3 it can build that track's pinnacle, which helps the whole kingdom. There's only one of each pinnacle in the realm.

What a settlement needs

Every settlement has to eat, and each size expects certain things to be built:

Size Food need Needs
Village 1 Huts, Campfire, Shrine
Town 2 Food +1, Faith +1, a Watch starter
City 4 Food +2, Faith +2, Watch +1, Walls +1, Public Works +1
Metropolis 6 Food +3, Faith +2, Watch +2, Walls +2, Public Works +2, Governance +1

Food stages lower a settlement's own food need. Farms on the map feed the rest, and the treasury buys whatever they can't cover.

Trouble

A settlement that's missing something it needs invites trouble. Each month it may draw a trouble card about it: hungry families where there's no food, a doom-preaching prophet where there's no shrine, raiders where a city has no walls. Build what's missing and that trouble stops.

Tenements (Homes +1) are cramped and dirty: while they're a settlement's best homes, crime is more likely there and crime checks there are harder. Building Neighborhoods ends it.

A new kingdom gets three quiet months before any trouble.

More settlements

The Viceroy's Found a Settlement project starts a new village in a claimed hex. Tell the GM where; once they mark it on the map, it appears on the Settlements tab. New villages start bare, so build their starters first.

The Settlement Tracks page lists every stage of every track, and every trouble card.
