First step: the capital

A new kingdom starts with one job: found the capital. Until it has one, the desks stay quiet and no projects can start.

The Ruler's player (or any player, if nobody holds the Ruler's seat yet) clicks Found the capital on the Realm, Desks or Settlements tab:

  • Give it a name. If the GM has already drawn a settlement on the map, you can pick that one instead.
  • Tick On a river, lake or swamp if it is. Some buildings (a mill, a pier) only pay off by the water.
  • Tell the GM where it stands. A short note in the window goes into the GM's task.

It costs nothing and happens at once. The GM claims the hex and draws the village on the map.

The Settlements tab

Each settlement shows its urban grid: nine blocks of four lots. Built lots show their building, homes are green, and grey blocks aren't open yet.

A settlement grows on its own as soon as it qualifies (kingdom level, lots built, and enough homes), with a line in chat.

Building

Build here on a settlement (or Start project on the Viceroy's desk) shows what fits: buildings up to the kingdom's level that fit in the free lots. Each line shows the cost in BP, the work days, the lots it takes, and what it gives. It's a project like any other: delegate it or handle it personally.

  • Upgrades. Some buildings upgrade another (houses upgrade a tenement; a temple upgrades a shrine). You can build either fresh, or as an upgrade: the upgrade replaces the old building, reuses its lots and costs the difference.
  • Edifices and infrastructure go up once per settlement. Infrastructure (paved streets, walls) takes no lots.
  • When it's done it takes its lots straight away. Some buildings lower Unrest or add Fame when they're finished; a few add Ruin.

What buildings do

  • Skill bonuses. Most buildings give an item bonus to the kingdom skill of the activity they help (a mill helps Agriculture, a shrine Folklore). It counts on any check for a card with that skill, by an advisor or by a character handling it personally: from the settlement the card is about, or otherwise the realm's best settlement. Several of the same building stack, up to the settlement's best building bonus. Different buildings don't stack; the best counts.
  • Homes. A settlement needs one lot of homes (houses, tenements, inns and other residential buildings) for each block it uses. Without them it's overcrowded: +1 Unrest every month until you build more homes, and it can't grow.
  • Food. The settlement's size sets its food needs. A stockyard, or a mill by the water, lowers it.
