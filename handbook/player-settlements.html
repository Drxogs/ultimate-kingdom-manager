First step: the capital

A new kingdom starts with one job: found the capital. Until it has one, the desks stay quiet and no projects can start. The GM does this while setting up the kingdom; tell them where you'd like it and what to call it.

The capital starts with the three basic starters: Huts (Homes), a Campfire (Food) and a Shrine (Faith).

The Settlements tab

Each settlement has a panorama at the top: a far-off view of the place in its own country. It starts as a ring of huts round a fire and fills up as the settlement grows. Below it is the working part:

  • Its food need, whether it's by the water, and anything it's missing for its size.
  • Tracks, folded under the picture (click to open; it stays the way you left it). Inside are the skill bonuses the settlement gives and one row per track: what stands now (its stage, its description and what it gives), what comes next, its cost and time, and a Build or Upgrade button for whoever holds that track's seat.

Tracks

A settlement grows along twelve tracks, two for each realm stat. Each track is a straight line: a starter, then +1, +2 and +3.

Stat Tracks

Culture (Counselor) Faith, Performance
Economy (Treasurer) Commerce, Industry
Relations (Emissary) Governance, Watch
Military (General) Walls, Army
Arcane (Magister) Learning, Public Works
  • Homes sets the size. Huts make a village, Tenements a town (kingdom level 3), Neighborhoods a city (level 9) and Boroughs a metropolis (level 15).
  • Other tracks go at most one stage past Homes, so a village can build a +1 stage, a town a +2.
  • Bonuses. Stage +1, +2 or +3 gives that much as an item bonus to its skills on checks for cards about that settlement (or, for cards about nowhere in particular, the realm's best settlement). Starters only open the track. Homes gives no bonus (it sets the size); every other skill comes from exactly one track:

- Perks. Many stages do more: markets and inns earn BP each week, workshops make projects here cheaper, a church lowers Unrest when built, a magistrate's court wears down Crime every month. Each row says what it gives.

Building a stage

Press Build or Upgrade on a track's row. It becomes a project card on that track's desk, rolled with that stat's skills: delegate it or handle it personally like any project. If the council is on, you can put it to the council instead.

Stage Cost Work
Starter 2 BP 3 days
+1 8 BP 7 days
+2 25 BP 14 days
+3 60 BP 21 days
Pinnacle 120 BP 28 days

Discounts (workshops, smithies, a foundry) are already taken off the cost shown.

Pinnacles

A metropolis with a track at +3 can build that track's pinnacle: a University, a Monastery, a Great Bridge. Each does something kingdom-wide. There's only one of each pinnacle in the kingdom, so pick where it stands.

What a settlement needs

Each size expects some tracks to be built:

Size Food need Needs
Village 1 Huts, Campfire, Shrine
Town 2 Food +1, Faith +1, a Watch starter
City 4 Food +2, Faith +2, Watch +1, Walls +1, Public Works +1
Metropolis 6 Food +3, Faith +2, Watch +2, Walls +2, Public Works +2, Governance +1

Food stages lower the settlement's own food need. Each week the realm's farms are set against the total food need of all its settlements, and the treasury pays for any shortfall. Anything missing shows on the tab in red.

Trouble

A settlement that's missing something its size needs is open to trouble. At the start of each month it has about a one-in-three chance, for each missing track, of drawing a trouble card about it: hungry families with no food track, a prophet preaching doom with no faith, raiders when a city has no walls. Trouble cards are ordinary problem cards on the right desk, marked Trouble. Build the missing stage and that trouble stops coming.

  • Tenements (Homes +1 as the top stage) make crime likelier: they can draw a Pickpocket Ring or a Shanty Fire, and crime checks there take −1 (dirty streets). Building Neighborhoods ends it.
  • An easy start. A new kingdom draws no trouble cards for its first three months. The tab shows how long is left.
  • Some trouble cards give the ruler a favour on a critical success: a one-use reward held until spent.

More settlements

The Viceroy's Found a Settlement project starts a new village in a claimed hex. Name it and tell the GM where. New villages start bare: build their starters first. A village the GM draws on the Kingmaker map joins the Settlements tab as soon as the map is saved.
