Cards land on the desk of the seat they belong to. Each card shows what it's about, which skill it uses, where it is (if anywhere), and how long you have.

You can act for your own character's seat and any seat you supervise. A seat nobody at the table plays (held by an NPC, or run by a companion or a hired advisor with no supervising player) says any player: anyone can handle its cards, start its projects and make its choices.

Kinds of card

  • Problem (red): something going wrong. If nobody deals with it before its deadline, it counts as a failure.
  • Opportunity (green): a chance for something good. If you ignore it, it just goes away.
  • Desk matter: a smaller problem or opportunity. One arrives every week, on whichever desk has been quietest.
  • Audience (blue): a petitioner wants something from your seat. You answer in person; there's no roll.
  • Project (gold): work someone started on purpose, like a road or a new temple.
  • Trouble (red, tagged Trouble): a problem a settlement brought on itself by missing something it needs, like a riot where there's no food. See Settlements and Tracks.

Special cards stand out in chat too: trouble, projects, desk matters, audiences and story events each have a coloured edge and a small label above the title.

Some cards are ongoing: they come back every month until you get the result that ends them (hover the Ongoing tag to see which). Taking one to the party works the same way: if the result the GM records doesn't end it, it comes back next month. Some have stages: find the cult first, then root it out.

What you can do with a card

  • Delegate: hand it to your seat's advisor. They roll the kingdom's skill. The advisor works on one thing at a time.
  • Handle personally: pick anyone in the party and one of their own skills. A success counts as a critical success, and a critical success also earns the kingdom Fame. The character is busy until the work is done.
  • Borrowing someone: if the character doesn't hold or supervise this desk, they're lent to it. Until the work is done, both this desk and the character's own desk take no other work: no delegating, no handling personally, no rank-ups. A chat message says who was lent, and that character's player makes the roll (and can spend their own hero point on a reroll). Talk to them first. Either player can end it early by calling the work back (the ↺ button on the card).
  • Take to the party (dragon icon): some problems can become an adventure instead. The deadline pauses until the GM records how it went.
  • Hear them (audiences): read what the petitioner wants and choose an answer. Every answer shows its effects before you pick.
  • Call back: stop work on a card and free up whoever was on it.
  • Possible outcomes: open this on any card to see what each result would do before you commit.

When the GM isn't online

A note at the top of the Desks tab tells you. You can read your desk, but nothing can be done until the GM is back.

Deadlines and work time

Major events and trouble cards give you 30 days, desk matters and audiences 14. Work takes 3 to 9 days depending on how hard the card is (projects say how long when you start them). If the work will finish after the deadline, the deadline still counts.
