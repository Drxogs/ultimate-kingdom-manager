Cards land on the desk of the seat they belong to. Each one says what's going on, which skills can deal with it, where it is, and how long you have.

Kinds of card

  • Problems go wrong if they're ignored: when the deadline passes, the failure result happens.
  • Opportunities are chances for something good. Ignore one and it simply goes away.
  • Desk matters are smaller problems and opportunities. One turns up each week on whichever desk has been quietest.
  • Audiences are petitioners asking your seat for something. You choose an answer; there's no roll.
  • Projects are work someone started on purpose: a road, a farm, a new temple.
  • Trouble comes from a settlement that's missing something it needs, such as a bread riot where there's no food.

Some cards are ongoing and come back each month until you get the result that ends them. After each try the card rests for three weeks (the card shows when it's back), then has two weeks to be handled before it counts as missed. Some come in stages: find the cult first, then root it out.

Dealing with a card

  • Delegate: hand it to your seat's advisor, who rolls the kingdom's skill. An advisor works on one thing at a time.
  • Handle personally: send someone from the party, using one of their own skills. A critical success earns the kingdom Fame, and the player can reroll with a hero point. The character is busy until the work is done.
  • Borrowing a character: anyone in the party can take on any desk's card. If it isn't their own desk, both desks are tied up until the work is done, so talk to that player first. Their player makes the roll.
  • Take to the party: some problems can become an adventure. The deadline pauses until the GM records how it went.
  • Hear them: answer an audience. Every answer shows what it will do before you choose.
  • Call back: stop work on a card and free whoever was on it.
  • Possible outcomes: see what each result would do before you commit.

Time

Major events and trouble give you 30 days; desk matters and audiences 14. Work takes 3 to 9 days depending on how hard the card is. If the work won't be done by the deadline, the deadline still counts.

Everything happens through the GM's game, so when no GM is online you can read your desk but not act on it.
