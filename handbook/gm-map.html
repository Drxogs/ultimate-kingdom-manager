The map stays yours

Claims, roads, farmland, work sites and settlements are still marked with the Kingmaker module's own hex tools. Ultimate Kingdom Manager reads the map (to place events, count farms and work sites, find frontier hexes) and never edits it.

GM tasks

When something needs doing by hand, it goes on the GM tasks list on the Realm tab and is whispered to you:

  • A project finished: mark the claim, road, farmland, work site or building.
  • An event result the module can't apply itself: destroyed farmland, a structure turned to rubble, a leader out of action.

Tasks with a place have a jump-to-hex button. Tick them off when done.

Map pins

Events tied to a hex or settlement get a pin on the Stolen Lands map: red for problems, gold for opportunities, a house for projects. Pins link to your GM-only journal pages, so players don't see them.

To show players where something is, click the eye button on the card. The pin then links to a short public page in the Kingdom Notices journal (what it is, which desk, and the deadline), so players can see and open it. Click again to hide it. Projects are always shown. When a card is resolved its pin and notice go away (or the pin greys out, a setting).

The hex panel

When you open a hex in the Kingmaker hex panel, a Kingdom line lists the cards active on that hex. Players see revealed cards and projects only.

Advisor warnings

When a party token on the Stolen Lands map moves into a zone two or more levels above the party, the Warden's advisor (or the General's, or the Magister's) says something in chat, once per zone until the party levels up. The Advisor warnings setting makes it subtle, blunt (names the levels), or off.

Level gravity

Events, desk matters and quests prefer zones within about one level of the party, so the work tends to be where the party should be. Projects in zones well above the party's level cost more and take longer. Threats tend to avoid hexes the General has fortified.
