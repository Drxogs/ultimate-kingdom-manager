The kingdom has six realm stats, one per seat, each fed by two or three kingdom skills:

Seat Realm stat Skills
Emissary Relations Politics, Intrigue, Statecraft
Counselor Culture Arts, Folklore
General Military Warfare, Defense
Magister Arcane Magic, Scholarship, Engineering
Treasurer Economy Trade, Industry, Boating
Viceroy Community Agriculture, Wilderness, Exploration

Filling the bar

  • A roll with a stat's skills adds points: +3 for a critical success, +2 for a success, nothing for a failure, −1 for a critical failure.
  • A problem left past its deadline costs 2; answering an audience adds 1.
  • Every seat with someone in it gains a little each week on its own. An empty seat gains nothing.
  • A stat that's fallen well behind catches up faster.

Ranking up

Each rank takes 20 points, and a stat can't rank higher than half the party's level, rounded up. When the bar is full, press Begin rank-up on the Realm tab. It costs about two weeks of income and keeps that seat's advisor busy for 14 days. Then the seat's player picks one of three rewards. The eye next to a stat's rank shows the next three choices at any time.

A stat's rank sets how well its advisors roll its skills: trained at ranks 0–1, expert at 2–3, master at 4–7 and legendary at 8–10, plus an attribute bonus that grows with the kingdom's level. Kept at the rank cap, an advisor rolls about as well as your best skill. Economy's rank also adds 1 BP a week per rank.

When the kingdom is founded, the party picks two stats to start at rank 1.

The ruler

The ruler has three powers, each usable once a month from the Realm tab:

  • Royal Decree: roll a waiting result again and keep the better of the two.
  • Royal Court: hold court in the capital. Unrest −1, or −2 in anarchy.
  • Royal Charge: a project underway finishes a week sooner.

Some results also give the ruler a favour, a one-time reward kept until it's spent.

Stepping down. When the realm is in anarchy, the ruler can give up the crown to another player character. The people take it as a fresh start and Unrest falls to 0. The cost is the old reign: all Fame, every favour and this month's royal powers are gone. The old ruler can never be crowned again. It's meant to be a big scene at the table, and a way out when nothing else will work.

The land

The Realm tab shows what your claimed hexes hold: roads, settlements, farmland, work sites, commodities and landmarks. Farms feed your settlements and work sites earn BP. The GM marks them on the map, and the list follows.

Fame and Unrest

Fame is the realm's reputation, up to 3. It grows by 1 each month, and more with great results.

  • Spend 1 Fame to reroll a waiting result.
  • Spend all of it to stop a disaster: when a Ruin would add a penalty, or Unrest would reach 20, the GM will ask.

Unrest is how unhappy the realm is. It's a penalty on every kingdom check: −1 from 1 Unrest, −2 from 5, −3 from 10, −4 from 15. The Counselor's Quell Unrest project and the ruler's Royal Court bring it down; don't let it run away.

  • 10 or more: each month brings Ruin, and a flat check to keep hold of a hex.
  • 20 or more: anarchy. Every check is one step worse until Unrest falls below 20. Quell Unrest and Royal Court work twice as well, and the ruler can step down.

Ruin (Corruption, Crime, Decay, Strife) builds up from bad results. When one fills, it leaves a lasting −1 on its skills and starts again from zero.

Rewards that don't stack

PF2e bonuses of the same type don't add up; only the biggest counts. If a reward's bonus wouldn't help because you already have a bigger one, you get rerolls for those checks instead, and the choice tells you so.

Critical successes

Every critical success on a card also brings a small windfall of BP.
